# Dancing with the SARs: project plan

NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026. Plan written 2026-11-09.
The official Resources tab opens Friday 2026-11-13; revisit the data section then.

## 1. The idea

**One radar engine, three dances, one country.**
A web app where anyone can pick a place in Algeria (or anywhere), scrub through
NISAR radar passes, and see what changed, explained in plain language.

It opens on three guided stories from Algeria in 2026, then lets the user
explore freely with the same tools.

| Act | Change | Where | Radar signal we expect |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Fire | August 2026 wildfires | Bejaia, Jijel, Tizi Ouzou, Skikda | Cross-pol (HV) drops where canopy burned |
| 2. Water | Autumn rains filling chotts, sebkhas, dams | Candidates: Chott Ech Chergui, Sebkha d'Oran, El Kala wetlands, Saharan wadis | HH and HV both drop over open water |
| 3. Farming | Irrigated circles and field cycles | Candidates: El Oued, Biskra, Adrar | HV rises and falls with crop growth |

Stretch act: ground deformation from interferograms (GUNW). Only if the three
acts are finished early.

### Why this should stand out

- **Combines change types without tripling the work.** All three acts use the
  same product (GCOV backscatter) and the same change maths. Only the
  thresholds and the story text differ.
- **A real, recent event.** The late-August 2026 fires killed at least 12
  people in the north-east. Pre-fire and post-fire NISAR passes both fall
  inside the calibrated data window.
- **Local knowledge.** The challenge page explicitly rewards personal context
  for a home region.
- **Live, not a slideshow.** The backend reads NISAR on demand, so judges can
  pan to their own city and it still works.
- **Explains the radar.** Each view answers "what am I looking at and why",
  which the challenge asks for (drivers, polarizations, future uses).

Working name idea: **Raqs** (Arabic for "dance"). Optional.

## 2. Data (checked 2026-11-09)

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| Source | NISAR L-band, from ASF DAAC / NASA Earthdata, free |
| Main product | L2 GCOV: terrain-corrected backscatter, HDF5, group `science/LSAR/GCOV/grids/frequencyA` |
| Validated data | Acquisitions from 2026-10-21 onward |
| Provisional (calibrated) | 2026-06-17 to about 2026-10-23 |
| Beta (uncalibrated) | Subset from 2025-10-17 to 2026-01-20; avoid mixing with the above |
| Known gap | No data 2026-07-27 to 2026-08-10 (instrument) |
| Repeat | 12 days per track, so roughly 10 to 12 dates per site so far |
| Access | Earthdata Login required to read files; searching is open |
| Tools | `asf_search`, `earthaccess`, `xarray` with `h5netcdf`, `titiler-cmr` |

### Spike results, fire area (2026-11-09)

Searched NASA's catalog for GCOV over lon 3.8 to 7.2, lat 36.3 to 37.1.
Script: `spike/find_passes.py`. Figure: `spike/fire_check_hv.png`.

- **67 passes, all dual-pol HH+HV.** Act 1 can use HV as planned.
- **Six tracks, each with 9 or 10 dates** from late June to late October,
  every 12 days, with one skipped date in the instrument gap.

| Track (stack) | Covers roughly | Last pass before fires | First pass after |
|---|---|---|---|
| 109 descending | Bejaia, Jijel, Collo, Skikda | 20 Aug | 1 Sep |
| 101 ascending | Jijel to Annaba | 20 Aug | 1 Sep |
| 029 ascending | Tizi Ouzou to Jijel | 15 Aug | 8 Sep (27 Aug is mid-event) |
| 037 descending | Algiers to Bejaia | 15 Aug | 8 Sep (27 Aug is mid-event) |
| 008 descending | Skikda to Tunisia | 13 Aug | 6 Sep (25 Aug is mid-event) |
| 130 ascending | Algiers, Tizi Ouzou west | 22 Aug | 3 Sep |

- **Signal found.** Comparing NASA's preview images on track 109 (median of
  5 passes before vs 5 after) shows compact patches where HV dropped and
  stayed low after 20 August, in the coastal mountains from Bejaia to
  Skikda. Inside three sample patches, brightness was flat across the five
  earlier passes and stepped down on 1 September.
- **Not yet validated.** The previews are 8-bit, contrast-stretched and
  coarse, so this is a visual indication only. It still needs checking
  against FIRMS fire detections and Sentinel-2, and redoing on the real
  calibrated data.
- **Files are large:** 1.9 GB (20 MHz mode) to 5.3 GB (40 MHz mode) each.
  Downloading whole files is out; the backend must read small windows.
- **Preview PNGs are public** (no login), about 1.5 MB per polarization.
  They can serve as an instant low-resolution layer while full data loads.
- **Validated collection** has only one pass here so far (24 Oct); nearly
  everything is in the provisional collection `NISAR_L2_GCOV_PROVISIONAL_V1`.

### Spike results, real data (2026-11-09, with Earthdata token)

Read track 109 files for 20 Aug and 1 Sep remotely, without downloading
them. Scripts: `spike/rangefile.py`, `spike/window_read.py`.
Figure: `spike/fire_check_real.png`.

| Item | Result |
|---|---|
| Token | Works as a bearer token; expires 2027-01-08 |
| Grid | 34200 x 34776 pixels, 10 m spacing, UTM zone 31N (EPSG:32631) |
| Layers in `frequencyA` | `HHHH`, `HVHV`, `mask`, `numberOfLooks`, `rtcGammaToSigmaFactor` |
| Storage | 512 x 512 chunks, gzip, so windows can be read on their own |
| `frequencyB` | Same scene at 80 m, 4275 x 4347 pixels: a ready-made overview layer |
| Open a file | About 8 s (21 MB of metadata) |
| Read a 20 km x 20 km window at 10 m | About 14 s and 20 MB per polarization |
| Grid alignment | Identical origin on both dates, so no resampling between passes |

Measured from a home connection in Algeria. A server in AWS us-west-2
should be several times faster.

- **Real signal.** In the test window, HV dropped by more than 3 dB over
  roughly 2,650 ha between the two passes (40 m cells, simple threshold).
  The patches are compact and follow the terrain.
- **Still not validated as fire.** The threshold count is not a burned-area
  estimate. Checking against FIRMS and Sentinel-2 is the next science step.

Design consequences:

- Window reads take 10+ seconds, so the cache and pre-warmed story areas
  are required, not optional.
- Use three zoom tiers: public preview PNG (instant), `frequencyB` at 80 m
  (overview), `frequencyA` at 10 m (detail, on demand).
- Because grids align across dates, a time stack is a plain array stack.

Still to settle:

- Polarizations over the Sahara sites for act 3.

### Backend status (2026-11-09)

Build step 1 is done and tested on real data. Run instructions are in
`README.md`.

- **Changed from the plan:** tiles are served by our own chunk reader, not
  `titiler-cmr`. Fetching compressed chunks in parallel cut a 20 km window
  from about 14 s to about 2.5 s, with far fewer dependencies.
- **Working:** scene search, pass details with preview corners, preview
  proxy, backscatter tiles (HH, HV, false colour), change tiles, point time
  series.
- **Measured:** first use of a pass about 10 s; whole 80 m layer about 6 s;
  cached tiles about 60 ms; a ten-pass time series 53 s cold, about 2 s warm
  (slower than expected for cached data; not yet profiled).
- **First real time series** (lon 6.0608, lat 36.7219, track 109, 100 m
  radius): HV between -12.5 and -13.2 dB on the five passes up to 20 Aug,
  then -14.4 dB on 1 Sep and about -15.5 dB by October. HH stayed within
  about 1 dB. Consistent with canopy loss; still not checked against FIRMS.
- **Not built yet:** area statistics (`/stats`), story presets (`/stories`),
  a warm-up command for story areas, multi-date median change, tests.

Supporting layers (all free): Sentinel-2 optical imagery for context, NASA
FIRMS fire detections to validate burn scars, wilaya boundaries, population
grid, ESA WorldCover land cover.

## 3. Architecture

```
Browser (Next.js + MapLibre)
        |  tiles, JSON
        v
API (Python, FastAPI)
  - scene search      -> NASA CMR / ASF
  - backscatter tiles -> titiler-cmr (xarray backend) reading GCOV
  - change engine     -> numpy/xarray on an area-of-interest stack
  - cache             -> subset stacks on disk (Zarr) + rendered tiles
        |
        v
NISAR GCOV in Earthdata Cloud (AWS us-west-2)
```

**Frontend:** Next.js (TypeScript), MapLibre GL, Tailwind, a small chart
library. Hosted on Vercel.

**Backend:** FastAPI in a Docker container, with `titiler-cmr` mounted for
tiles and our own routes for change and time series. Best hosted in AWS
us-west-2 next to the data; can run on a laptop for the demo.

**Cache is what makes "live" usable.** First visit to a new area is slow
(seconds to tens of seconds). The three story areas are warmed in advance so
the demo is instant.

### API

| Route | Returns |
|---|---|
| `GET /scenes?bbox&start&end` | Available passes: date, track, direction, polarizations |
| `GET /tiles/{z}/{x}/{y}?date&pol` | Backscatter tile (HH, HV, or colour composite) |
| `GET /change/{z}/{x}/{y}?before&after&mode` | Change tile (fire, water, crops, generic) |
| `GET /timeseries?lon&lat` | Backscatter in dB per date for a point or polygon |
| `GET /stats?polygon&before&after` | Changed area in hectares |
| `GET /stories` | The curated acts: location, dates, text |

### Change engine

1. Read a stack of passes for the area, same track and direction only.
2. Convert to dB and reduce speckle (multi-temporal filter).
3. Compute log-ratio between "before" and "after" (median of several dates
   each, where available).
4. Apply per-mode rule: fire = HV decrease over vegetation; water = low HH
   and HV; crops = HV seasonal amplitude; generic = any strong change.
5. Clean up small speckle blobs, report area.

Also: a three-date colour composite (red = before, green/blue = after), the
classic way to see change in SAR at a glance.

## 4. Interface

- **Map** with a before/after swipe handle.
- **Timeline** of passes with a play button, so the surface animates.
- **Layer switch:** HH, HV, colour composite, change overlay, optical.
- **Click anywhere** for a backscatter chart over time.
- **Draw an area** to get hectares changed.
- **Story mode:** each act is a short guided tour with text beside the map.
- **"What am I seeing?"** cards explaining roughness, water, polarization.
- Stretch: Arabic and French interface alongside English.

## 5. Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| HV not acquired over a chosen site | Check in step 0; swap site or use HH-only rules |
| Remote reads too slow | Cache subsets; pre-warm story areas; host backend in us-west-2 |
| Fire scar signal weak or ambiguous at L-band | Validate against FIRMS and Sentinel-2; if weak, lead with water instead |
| Autumn 2026 flooding not well documented | Act 2 uses seasonal water bodies, not a specific flood event |
| Provisional vs validated products differ | Keep each comparison inside one collection where possible |
| Scope too large | Acts are independent; ship act 1 end to end before starting act 2 |

## 7. Upgrade: advanced AI and animated app (added 2026-11-09)

Decision by the team: go beyond the baseline plan with a deep-learning
model and a cinematic, animated interface.

Machine available for training: GTX 1660 Ti (6 GB), 16 GB RAM, 42 GB free
disk. That fits a compact model trained from scratch, not fine-tuning a
large foundation model. Training data must stay under about 10 GB.

### 7.1 AI stack, three layers

**Layer 1: perception. A spatio-temporal network that reads the whole
radar time series.**

- Input: every pass of a track as a stack (HH and HV per date) in patches.
- Architecture: U-Net with a temporal attention encoder (U-TAE family),
  roughly one million parameters. The attention weights show which pass
  the model relied on, which gives the date of change and makes the model
  explainable on screen.
- Output per pixel: stable, burned, water gained, water lost, vegetation
  growth, vegetation loss.
- Labels by cross-sensor supervision ("optical teaches radar"): burn
  severity, water index and greenness change computed from free Sentinel-2
  imagery on clear days. Once trained, the model needs radar only, so it
  works at night and through smoke and cloud.
- Evaluation on tiles the model never saw, reported as IoU and F1, and
  compared against two baselines: a fixed dB threshold and gradient-boosted
  trees. The deep model ships only if it beats them.

**Layer 2: self-supervised rhythm model (stretch).**

- Hide one pass, train the network to reconstruct it from the others. No
  labels needed, so it can train on any region.
- Where reconstruction fails, the surface "missed a step": an anomaly map
  that powers free exploration anywhere in the world.

**Layer 3: reasoning. A Claude agent that drives the map.**

- The user asks in Arabic, French or English ("what burned near Jijel in
  August?"). The agent calls our own API as tools (search, change, stats,
  time series), moves the camera, and narrates the answer.
- It may state only numbers our API returned. Output is labelled as AI.
- Needs an Anthropic API key in `.env`.

**Serving:** model exported to ONNX and run by the backend per tile, with
results cached like any other layer.

Honest risks:

- With about ten dates and weak labels, the deep model may not beat the
  simple baselines. The comparison decides; the baseline is the fallback.
- Fire labels come from one event in one region. Accuracy elsewhere is
  unproven and the app must say so.
- GTX 16-series cards are unreliable with half-precision training, so
  training runs in full precision and is slower.

### 7.2 Animated web app

Stack: Next.js, TypeScript, MapLibre GL (globe and 3D terrain), deck.gl for
GPU layers, Motion for interface animation, Tailwind.

- **Opening:** rotating globe with NISAR's tracks, then a fly-in to Algeria.
- **3D terrain:** radar draped over the Kabylie mountains, tilted camera.
- **The dance:** passes cross-fade on the GPU as the timeline plays; changed
  pixels pulse in time with the pass that changed them.
- **Story mode:** scroll-driven acts with choreographed camera moves.
- **Compare:** draggable swipe between any two passes or layers.
- **Charts:** time series that draw themselves when a point is clicked.
- **Ask:** the agent panel, with the map responding as it answers.
- Respects reduced-motion settings; usable on a phone.

### 7.3 Order

1. Frontend core with globe, terrain, timeline animation and swipe.
2. Label and patch pipeline (Sentinel-2 labels, NISAR stacks).
3. Baselines, then the deep model, then evaluation.
4. Model serving and the AI layer in the map.
5. Agent panel.
6. Story mode polish, self-supervised stretch, submission.

### 7.4 Frontend status (2026-11-10)

First version built and checked in the browser on real data: intro globe
and fly-in, 3D terrain, HV / HH / colour layers, cross-fading timeline,
change overlay, point inspector with chart, phone layout.

Changed from the plan: no deck.gl yet. MapLibre raster layers with opacity
transitions were enough for the cross-fade and the pulsing overlay.

Not built yet: story mode with scroll-driven acts, swipe comparison, NISAR
track animation on the globe, per-pixel pulse timed to the pass that
changed it, Arabic and French text, a warm-up command so the first
playback is instant, automated tests.

Known rough edges: the first playback of a track takes a couple of
minutes; the change overlay reloads on every frame during playback.

### 7.5 AI status (2026-11-10)

Story mode is deferred by the team's choice; AI came first.

- **Working, tested on real data:** analysis jobs over a whole time stack
  (`POST /analysis`). A least-squares change-point test finds the date and
  size of the step in every cell; physical rules name it. The fire coast at
  80 m (1.67 million ha) takes about 30 s cold and 10 s warm. Most flagged
  area is dated to the 1 September pass. Shown in the app's AI analysis tab
  with "what changed" and "when" layers and hectares per class.
- **Written and smoke-tested on synthetic data only:** the ChangeUTAE
  network, training loop, baselines and ONNX export (`ml/`). The backend
  loaded an exported model and ran it on real radar, with untrained weights.
- **Written, never run:** Sentinel-2 label builder (`ml/sardance/labels.py`)
  and the Claude assistant (`backend/app/assistant.py`, no API key yet).
- **Not done:** training on real labels, GPU run, validation against FIRMS,
  self-supervised stretch, automated tests.
- **Changed from the plan:** the model has about 480,000 parameters, not a
  million. The agent uses a hand-written tool loop with four tools.
- Usage and training steps are in `ml/README.md`.

### 7.6 Trained model (2026-11-10)

- **Validation of the fire signal:** burn scars from Sentinel-2 (about
  54,600 ha in a 62 x 38 km box near Jijel) match the NISAR HV drop in
  shape. Inside them the median HV change is -1.4 dB; 47% of cells fall by
  more than 1.5 dB, against 4% outside.
- **Model:** ChangeUTAE reaches 0.86 burned IoU on unseen test cells, against
  0.67 for gradient-boosted trees. Exported to `models/change_utae.onnx` and
  selectable in the app as "Neural model" at 20 m.
- **Limits:** one fire complex, one track, labels from an optical threshold;
  only "burned" is reliable; untested elsewhere. Details in `ml/README.md`.
- **Not done:** check against FIRMS or official perimeters, a second region,
  the in-app visual check of the neural option (the window was hidden).
